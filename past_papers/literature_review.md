## Thematic Analysis

### Theme 1: From Single Models to Hybrid Architectures
A clear trend across the reviewed papers is the shift from standalone models (ARIMA, simple LSTM) toward hybrid architectures that combine multiple techniques. Papers 1, 3, and 10 all demonstrate that combining a *decomposition step* (CEEMDAN or STL) with a *temporal model* (LSTM or Transformer) yields significant accuracy gains. The rationale is clear: decomposition pre-processes the non-stationary nature of air quality time series, while the deep model focuses on learning residual, stationary patterns. This pipeline is highly applicable to the Slovenian AQI dataset, which exhibits strong seasonal and diurnal periodicity.

### Theme 2: Capturing Spatial Dependencies via Graph Neural Networks
Papers 4, 5, and 6 all leverage the spatial topology of monitoring stations through Graph Neural Networks. The key insight is that air quality at any given station is not independent — it is influenced by upwind stations, nearby industrial clusters, and regional meteorological dynamics. Paper 5 goes further by treating space as a *continuous field* (rather than a discrete graph), achieving state-of-the-art results for inferring air quality at unmonitored locations. This approach is directly relevant to the dataset, as Slovenia's 21-station network could benefit from ST-GNN modelling to fill spatial gaps between stations.

### Theme 3: Regime-Based Analysis vs. Point Forecasting
While most papers focus on point forecasting (predicting exact pollutant concentrations), Papers 7 and 8 adopt a different paradigm: *classifying air quality regimes*. Paper 7 uses K-Means + Random Forest to identify four recurring pollution regimes in Cairo (traffic, dust, clean, and mixed), while Paper 8 applies Spectral Clustering to source apportionment. These regime-based analyses are more interpretable for policymakers and align with the product direction of a B2B compliance dashboard. Rather than showing raw predictions, a regime-based system can trigger targeted alerts ("High Dust Event Regime — industrial facilities should initiate protective protocols").

### Theme 4: Evaluation Diversity
Papers use a range of evaluation metrics. The most common are **RMSE**, **MAE**, and **MAPE**, with Papers 4 adding **POD** (Probability of Detection), **CSI** (Critical Success Index), and **FAR** (False Alarm Rate) — metrics borrowed from meteorological forecasting. For health applications, the latter three metrics are arguably more meaningful: a false alarm rate of 7.2% (Paper 4) is a tangible policy-relevant number.

## Research Gap Analysis

| Gap | Papers Identifying It | Relevance to Slovenian AQI Dataset |
|-----|-----------------------|------------------------------------|
| Lack of multi-city/cross-regional validation | Papers 1, 3, 7, 9 | The Slovenia dataset covers 21 stations across one region; cross-regional validation remains an open question |
| Insufficient incorporation of terrain & PBL height | Paper 4 | Slovenia has complex Alpine/Karst terrain; terrain-aware models could significantly improve accuracy |
| No real-time / live-feed integration | Papers 1 (addresses this via AWS), rest | The AQI dataset is historical; connecting to ARSO live API is the key gap to productize |
| Sparse treatment of hyperlocal sources (street-level) | Papers 5, 6 | Station-level data cannot resolve street-level variations; satellite-integrated models (Paper 4) partially address this |
| Limited interpretability of deep models | Papers 1, 2, 10 | Hybrid LSTM/Transformer models are black-boxes; Papers 7 and 8 (tree-based) offer better interpretability for policy use |

## Conclusion
The 10 papers collectively chart a progression from simple univariate LSTM forecasting to sophisticated, spatially-aware hybrid architectures. The most actionable insight for the Slovenian AQI dataset is:
1. **Use STL + TCN-BiLSTM (Paper 10)** as a strong baseline model — its architecture is directly applicable to PM10/PM2.5 hourly time series and should significantly outperform the naive persistence model.
2. **Adopt a GNN-based spatial model (Paper 6)** to exploit the 21-station network's geographic structure and fill spatial gaps.
3. **Add a regime-classification layer (Paper 7)** for a more policy-friendly, interpretable product output.

The combination of these three directions represents a realistic, well-validated research roadmap using this dataset.
